# Munger's Mental Models (Diagnostic Layer)

Function: risk surfacing, diagnosis sharpening, decision stress-testing.

Source lineage: Charlie Munger's mental models, applied to the strategy kernel from Richard Rumelt's Good Strategy / Bad Strategy. Companion to the Rumelt's Good Strategy Framework card.

## Purpose

Apply a small set of high-leverage mental models to improve diagnosis quality, identify critical risks, and prevent shallow or biased strategic conclusions under uncertainty.

## Core models (use as prompts, not theory)

### Inversion (failure-first analysis)

Reframe the question from "How do we succeed?" to "How does this fail catastrophically?"

Use it to identify dominant risks, fragilities, and fatal assumptions.

Kernel tie-in: strengthens Diagnosis by revealing the true problem structure before policy is set.

### Second-order effects

Map consequences beyond the immediate outcome. Ask "Then what?" at least twice.

Surface delayed costs, behavioral reactions, and systemic feedback loops.

Kernel tie-in: prevents policies that "work" short-term but undermine the strategy later.

### Three-dimensional / long-horizon thinking

Assess impacts across time, stakeholders, and system layers, not just the visible transaction.

Look for hidden damage, compounding effects, or path dependence.

Kernel tie-in: improves the realism of both Diagnosis and Guiding Policy.

### Circle of competence boundary check

Explicitly mark what is known, partially known, and unknown.

Flag where confidence exceeds evidence.

Kernel tie-in: prevents false clarity during diagnosis and overreach in policy design.

### Margin of safety

Assume forecasts are wrong. Build buffers into assumptions, timelines, resources, and expectations.

Kernel tie-in: converts insight into robust action, not brittle plans.

### Bias and incentive scan

Check for incentive-caused bias, social proof, confirmation bias, or authority bias shaping conclusions.

Kernel tie-in: keeps the diagnosis honest; protects against narrative-driven strategy.

## When to invoke this card

- Before finalizing a Diagnosis
- When multiple options look "roughly equivalent"
- When stakes are high and information is incomplete
- During red-team or pre-mortem passes

## Failure modes this card prevents

- Treating goals as strategy
- Confusing motion with progress
- Ignoring dominant risks
- Over-trusting optimistic narratives
- Building elegant plans on weak assumptions

## Operating principle

Strategy fails less often from lack of intelligence than from failure to see how it breaks.
