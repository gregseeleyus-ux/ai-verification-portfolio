# Collaborative War-Gaming

Status: CONCEPT. Discussed once as an alternative design. Nothing has been built or run.

## What it is

A way to set research and intelligence priorities in which the stakeholders who will use the findings help produce the intelligence requirements. They do this through three stages: war-room sessions, research sprints, and a synthesis workshop. It is an alternative to an analyst-driven pipeline, where one analyst decides what matters and hands over finished products.

## When to use it

- The people who will act on the research disagree about what they need to know, or have not said.
- Buy-in to the findings matters as much as the findings themselves.
- The work fits policy, advocacy, consulting, research, or competitive analysis, where decision-makers can give time to shared sessions.

## How to run it

The concept names three stages, run in this order. None of them has been specified further.

1. **War-room sessions.** Stakeholders meet to state and rank the intelligence requirements: the questions the research must answer.
2. **Research sprints.** Short, bounded research runs work against the requirements from the sessions.
3. **Synthesis workshop.** Stakeholders and researchers review the sprint findings together and turn them into shared conclusions.

Not yet defined: who takes part, how often sessions run, how long sprints last, what format requirements are written in, and how the workshop records its decisions. Settle these on a first run before promoting the method past CONCEPT.

## Output

A set of intelligence requirements co-owned by the stakeholders, the sprint findings against those requirements, and the conclusions from the synthesis workshop.
