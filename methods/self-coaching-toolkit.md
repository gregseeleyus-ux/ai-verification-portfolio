# Self-Coaching Toolkit for High-Ambiguity Work

Status: DESIGNED (a complete reference toolkit exists and has been cited in diagnostic work; repeat operational use of the cards and forms is not documented)

Note: each card's "Basis" line summarizes the research the toolkit drew on. The sources are not cited or verified here; treat them as pointers for further reading, not established findings.

## What it is

A numbered library of self-coaching instruments for work where feedback is scarce, metrics are unclear, and there is no manager or coach to diagnose a stall. It has three parts: a diagnostic map that names the stall, fifteen Coaching Cards (CC-01 to CC-15) that interrupt it with a shift, three questions, and an action trigger, and fifteen Forms (F-01 to F-15) that hold the data for tracking over time. The underlying move is to switch from performance goals (prove you are good) to learning goals (get better at the skill) while the path is uncertain, and to ask for advice rather than feedback.

## When to use it

- You are in an undefined role or project and nobody is telling you how you are doing.
- You notice a stall: boredom, over-research, perfectionism, procrastination, overload, isolation, drift, or conflict with a stakeholder.
- You want to turn daily friction into measurable growth ("distance traveled") instead of a verdict on yourself.
- As a reference an AI assistant can load: paste the diagnostic map and the matching card or form, describe the situation, and ask it to walk you through the steps.

### 2. Pull the Coaching Card

Each card has a shift (the reframe), an inquiry (three questions, answered in order), and an action trigger (the thing you do immediately). The basis line names the research idea the card draws on.

**Category A: Start (unblocking)**

**CC-01 The Gamification Catalyst.** Basis: deliberate play and flow; add challenge to match skill.
- Shift: stop grinding; design a game.
- Inquiry: (1) What is the boring skill I must practice right now? (2) What constraint would make this harder or more interesting (time limit, forbidden words)? (3) What score am I trying to beat from my last attempt?
- Action trigger: set a timer for 15 minutes, create one arbitrary rule, go.

**CC-02 The Decision Compressor.** Basis: satisficing over maximizing.
- Shift: you are looking for the reversible choice, not the perfect one.
- Inquiry: (1) Is this decision reversible? If yes, speed up. (2) What is the cost of doing nothing for another day versus the cost of being wrong? (3) If I had to decide in 5 minutes, what would I choose?
- Action trigger: write down the 5-minute decision. Wait 1 hour. If no fatal flaw appears, execute.

**CC-03 The Imperfectionist's Pivot.** Basis: perfectionism does not track performance on complex tasks; favor learning speed.
- Shift: the goal is an 8/10 delivered today so you can get advice, not a 10/10.
- Inquiry: (1) What is the minimum viable quality for the next step? (2) Which flaw am I obsessing over that the end user likely won't notice? (3) Am I polishing to improve the work or to protect my ego?
- Action trigger: identify one flaw you will intentionally leave in. Ship it.

**CC-10 The Implementation Trigger.** Basis: implementation intentions.
- Shift: willpower is finite; triggers are automatic.
- Inquiry: (1) What is the specific situation (time or place) where I need to act? (2) What is the specific action? (3) How can I automate this with an If-Then structure?
- Action trigger: write "If [situation] happens, then I will [action]." Post it where you will see it.

**CC-13 The Pre-Mortem Projector.** Basis: the pre-mortem technique; legitimize doubt and turn worry into strategy.
- Shift: do not ask "what if this fails?"; assume it has failed and ask why.
- Inquiry: (1) Six months from now the project has failed badly. What was the specific cause? (2) What early warning sign did we ignore in week 1? (3) What one preventive action can I take today to remove that cause?
- Action trigger: list the top 3 fatal flaws and one mitigation for each.

**Category B: Grow (learning and mastery)**

**CC-05 The Pattern Breaker.** Basis: deliberate practice; separate doing the work from practicing the weak point.
- Shift: stop doing the work; start practicing the weak point.
- Inquiry: (1) Which sub-component of this task is the bottleneck? (2) If I could improve only one micro-skill this week, which one? (3) How can I get immediate feedback on just that sub-component?
- Action trigger: design a 20-minute drill that isolates only the bottleneck.

**CC-08 The Mistake Budgeter.** Basis: the mistake budget; errors are a resource to spend, not only a risk to avoid.
- Shift: you are not supposed to avoid mistakes; you are supposed to spend your budget.
- Inquiry: (1) What is my mistake budget for this project (for example, 10 errors)? (2) If I make a mistake here, is it fatal or merely instructional? (3) What is the distance-traveled value of this potential failure?
- Action trigger: commit to making 3 smart mistakes today in service of speed.

**CC-09 The Sponge Filter.** Basis: absorptive capacity; passive reading creates an illusion of competence.
- Shift: do not just consume; filter and apply.
- Inquiry: (1) Is this information confirming what I know or challenging me? (2) What is the one actionable insight from this source? (3) How can I test it today?
- Action trigger: summarize the insight in one sentence and apply it to a current task immediately.

**CC-14 The Energy Auditor.** Basis: managing energy, not only time.
- Shift: do not manage your time; manage your energy state.
- Inquiry: (1) Is my current fatigue physical, emotional, or mental? (2) Which tasks create energy for me and which drain it? (3) Can I align my hardest task with my peak window?
- Action trigger: schedule a recovery block, or switch to a high-energy task for 20 minutes.

**Category C: Connect (network)**

**CC-04 The Advice Architect.** Basis: asking for advice yields more critical and actionable input than asking for feedback.
- Shift: turn your critic into a coach.
- Inquiry: (1) Who has the expertise to help me improve future performance? (2) Instead of "How was it?", ask "What is one thing I could do to get closer to a 10 next time?" (3) Am I ready to listen without defending?
- Action trigger: send one advice request using the F-04 script.

**CC-06 The Load Shedder.** Basis: scaffolding and cognitive load; borrow external structure.
- Shift: you do not need to have the skill yet; you need to borrow the structure.
- Inquiry: (1) Who has solved this problem before? (2) What tool, template, or framework can I borrow to start? (3) Can I set up a commitment device (for example, a meeting) to force progress?
- Action trigger: identify one person or tool to act as a scaffold. Schedule a 15-minute check-in.

**CC-11 The Network Weaver.** Basis: the strength of weak ties and growth networks.
- Shift: do not wait to be discovered; build the bridge.
- Inquiry: (1) Who is a weak tie I haven't spoken to in 6 months who might have a fresh perspective? (2) How can I offer value to someone in my network today without asking for anything? (3) Who is scaffolding me, and whom am I scaffolding?
- Action trigger: send one dormant-tie reactivation message.

**CC-15 The Perspective Shifter.** Basis: perspective taking; move from positions to interests.
- Shift: do not argue your point; argue theirs.
- Inquiry: (1) What unwritten rule is driving the other person's behavior? (2) In their position, what would I be afraid of losing? (3) What does success look like for them in this interaction?
- Action trigger: rewrite your proposal from the stakeholder's perspective.

**Category D: Integrate (review and reflection)**

**CC-07 The Distance Calculator.** Basis: the distance-traveled metric; measure growth from the starting point.
- Shift: do not measure the peak; measure the climb.
- Inquiry: (1) Where was I 3 months ago on this skill versus now? (2) What obstacles have I navigated that don't show on the scoreboard? (3) If I kept this trajectory, where would I be in a year?
- Action trigger: write down three invisible wins from the last month.

**CC-12 The Pivot Point.** Basis: reflection-in-action and retrospectives.
- Shift: activity does not equal progress.
- Inquiry: (1) What did I set out to achieve this week versus what did I actually do? (2) Is the ladder I'm climbing leaning against the right wall? (3) What one thing should I stop doing to make room for a learning goal?
- Action trigger: cancel one low-value meeting or task.

### 3. Fill the Form

Forms are minimal on purpose, to avoid tracking fatigue. Blank versions are in the template block below.

- **F-01 Deliberate Play Designer:** task; the grind (what makes it hard or boring); game mechanic (time attack, accuracy score, or a constraint); target score; post-game review (did I beat it, fun factor 1 to 10).
- **F-02 Decision Journal:** decision; date, time, and state of mind; context; the choice and why; alternatives rejected; expected outcome with a confidence percentage; outcome review about 6 months later (what happened; right for the right reasons, or lucky?). Quick version: the dilemma, the good-enough choice, why it is reversible, the execute-by deadline.
- **F-03 Minimum Viable Output Checklist:** deliverable; the perfect version; the good-enough version (bare minimum to get advice); the flaws I accept; ship date.
- **F-04 Advice-Seeking Script Builder:** the person (credible, familiar with the work, cares about you); the project; the request (not "Let me know what you think" but "I'm working on improving [skill]. Could you review this and tell me one specific thing I could do differently next time to make it more effective? I'm not looking for praise; I want to improve my process."); advice received; how I will apply it in the next iteration.
- **F-05 Deliberate Practice Plan:** macro skill; sub-skills; the one sub-skill this week; the drill (for example, deliver the opening 10 times with zero filler words); feedback mechanism (how I will know I improved).
- **F-06 Cognitive Offload Map:** list every open loop; sort into Do Now, Defer (schedule), Delegate or Scaffold (who can help), Delete; name the one thing for the next hour.
- **F-07 Distance Traveled Log:** period; starting capability; obstacles (external and internal); strategy used; current capability (what I can do now that I couldn't then); growth rating 1 to 10. Fill monthly.
- **F-08 Error Forensics Report:** the error; the cause, found with 5 Whys and stated as a system failure, not a personal one; the lesson (the learning goal achieved); the system fix or new rule (for example, "next time, check X before Y").
- **F-09 Information Absorption Tracker:** source; key insight in one sentence; relevance to current work; one experiment to test it; result.
- **F-10 If-Then Planning Sheet:** goal; the critical obstacle (what usually stops me); one or more IF [obstacle or cue] THEN [specific action] lines (for example, IF I feel stuck on the blank page, THEN I write one bad paragraph just to start).
- **F-11 Support Network Audit:** current challenge; network by role (Coaches give advice, Cheerleaders give support, Connectors know people, Critics challenge me); gap analysis (which role is missing); outreach plan.
- **F-12 Weekly Review and Reset:** wins (what I learned); stalls (where I got stuck, named from the diagnostic map); cognitive load check (intrinsic: was the work too hard, so I need scaffolding? extraneous: was the process too messy, so I need systems?); next week's learning goal.
- **F-13 Risk/Mitigation Worksheet:** proposed action; worst case (pre-mortem); probability (Low, Med, High); impact (Low, Med, High); mitigation; contingency plan.
- **F-14 Chronotype Activity Log:** time block; activity; energy 1 to 10; focus 1 to 10; pattern (when am I most effective for deep work?).
- **F-15 Stakeholder Empathy Map:** stakeholder; what they see (environment, market, problems); what they hear (bosses, peers, influencers); what they think and feel (fears, aspirations); what they say and do (public behavior); pain points; gain points.

### 4. Run the rhythm

**Daily (about 5 minutes total)**
1. Morning: pick one learning goal for the day (not "finish report" but "learn to write faster intros"). Set an implementation intention (F-10) for the day's hardest task. Optionally pick one character skill (proactivity, discipline, determination) to practice on purpose.
2. During the day, when you stall: stop, do not power through. Diagnose with the map. Pull the card. Execute its action trigger immediately.
3. Evening: brief reflection in F-12 (what happened, how it went, what next). If an error occurred, log it in F-08.

**Weekly (20 to 30 minutes, end or start of the week)**
1. Complete F-12.
2. Distance check (CC-07): capability now versus last week; skills gained, not just tasks done.
3. Review F-02 decision journals for any closed loops (decision made versus outcome).
4. Scaffold check (CC-06): did I try to solve everything alone? If so, schedule one advice call for next week using F-04.
5. Clear the F-06 offload map.

**First 14 days**
- Day 1: put the diagnostic map where you work.
- Day 2: baseline. Fill F-07 for the last 3 months.
- Days 3 to 5: use CC-04. Send 3 specific advice requests on a current project.
- Day 7: weekly review. Did you use the cards? Which stall appeared most often?
- Day 14: scaffolding check. Have you built a tool or template that makes next week easier? If not, build one with CC-06.

### 5. Measure

Use distance traveled as the leading indicator, since conventional metrics lag growth in ambiguous roles.
- Trajectory versus position: are you moving up the curve even if absolute results are not visible yet?
- Recovery speed: when you stall, how fast do you diagnose and fix it with the cards?
- Advice quality: is the advice you get becoming more specific over time? That means your F-04 questions are getting better.

### 6. Failure modes

1. **Tool fetish:** more time designing the system than doing the work. Fix: apply CC-03 to the toolkit itself; start with only CC-02, CC-04, and CC-10.
2. **Feedback relapse:** asking "How was that?" again and getting vague praise. Fix: script the question with F-04 before every review; never enter a review without a script.
3. **Goal anxiety:** setting performance goals in high-uncertainty zones and freezing. Fix: switch to a learning goal ("discover 3 ways this strategy fails" rather than "make it succeed").
4. **Lonely hero:** solving everything alone because it feels faster. Fix: force a CC-06 scaffolding check. If you have not asked for advice in 48 hours, you are probably stalling.

## Output

Per stall: a named diagnosis, the answered three-question inquiry, and one executed action trigger. Over time: a set of filled forms (decision journals, error forensics, distance logs, weekly resets) that show recurring stalls, closed decision loops, and growth from the starting point.
