# Guardian Sector Vocabulary Map

The user operates across several sectors using the same underlying analytical
logic with different surface vocabulary. When porting analysis between
sectors, translate automatically using this table. When the user uses a term
from one sector, recognize the underlying concept and apply it with
appropriate vocabulary for the current context.

---

## Core Concept Translation Table

| Universal Concept | Infrastructure Sales | Advocacy and Organizing | Legislative/Policy | Consulting | Nonprofit and Community |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk transfer to enable action** | Political insurance | Permission structure | Political cover for vote | Risk mitigation framing | Funder and board cover |
| **Person who can kill this** | Procurement gatekeeper | Organized opposition group or rival coalition | Committee chair / veto player | Client-side blocker | Lead funder or agency director |
| **Person who must move first** | County manager | Coalition lead / organizing director | Bill sponsor | Decision-maker with budget authority | Executive director or board chair |
| **What motivates the decision-maker** | Fear of blame > hope of gain | Winning the fight (vote, election, or policy change) | Re-election + legacy | Career risk reduction | Community pressure + funder confidence |

## Sector-Specific Context

### Infrastructure Sales
- **Deal cycle:** long public-sector cycles; plan around budget and procurement calendars
- **Key framing:** total cost, risk allocation, and who carries the financing
- **Decision dynamic:** Elected officials need "political insurance":
  if the project fails, the private partner absorbs the blame, not the official
- **Common blockers:** Procurement process delays, anti-privatization sentiment,
  bond counsel opinions, incumbent vendor relationships

### Advocacy and Organizing
- **Key framing:** Authentic community engagement that produces both real
  community value AND usable policy substance
- **Decision dynamic:** Everything filters through "does this help win"
  balanced against "would the principal (coalition or organization)
  see this as substantive or as mere optics"
- **Common blockers:** Resource constraints, volunteer capacity,
  media narrative control, opponent counter-messaging

### Legislative/Policy Analysis
- **Key framing:** What survives constitutional challenge, what passes
  fiscal note review, what gets through committee
- **Decision dynamic:** Legislative arithmetic + committee chair power +
  lobbying group blocking records
- **Common blockers:** Entrenched organized opposition, fiscal scoring
  objections, constitutional vulnerability, session time constraints

### Consulting
- **Key framing:** Decision-ready intelligence that reduces risk for
  the client's specific decision context
- **Decision dynamic:** Client needs to feel confident enough to act,
  which means providing both the analysis AND the political/institutional
  cover to justify the decision
- **Common blockers:** Scope creep, client indecision, budget constraints,
  internal organizational politics

### Nonprofit and Community
- **Key framing:** Service-driven, nonpartisan where possible, anchored in
  real data about the populations served and their service gaps
- **Decision dynamic:** Funders and legislators respond to constituent
  stories + fiscal responsibility framing, not just moral arguments
- **Common blockers:** Competing budget priorities, coordination gaps
  across government levels and providers, agency and staff capacity
  limitations

## Usage Instructions

When the user says "run the stakeholder map" in an advocacy context, produce
a stakeholder map using advocacy vocabulary (coalition/opposition map,
winning the fight as motivator). When the user says "run the stakeholder map"
in an infrastructure context, use infrastructure vocabulary (procurement
gatekeeper, fear of blame as motivator). The underlying analysis is
identical; only the labels change.

When porting a finding across sectors (for example, applying a
legislative blocking analysis to an advocacy counter-strategy), translate
the vocabulary automatically and note the cross-sector application
explicitly so the user can verify the port is valid.
